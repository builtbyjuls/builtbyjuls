Product concept - Discovery and architecture

Arat?: plan together before anyone commits.

Arat? is designed to help groups agree on an outing, collect structured offers from private resorts and vehicle providers, and resolve changes before anyone treats the plan as final.

Role
Product strategy, workflow design, and architecture
Initial focus
Private resort outings with optional van transport
Status
Concept stage - Not implemented

The problem

Group plans fail between "tara" and "booked."

Planning an outing often means scattered chats, private budget concerns, uncertain headcount, and quotes with different conditions. A plan can look settled even when participants approved different dates, prices, or assumptions.

When one person withdraws or one supplier changes a condition, the organizer must work out who is affected and whether the original agreement still holds.

The story

Ten friends, one plan, and two withdrawals.

Ten friends want an overnight stay. They agree on two possible weekends, a private pool, enough beds, van transport, and a maximum personal share of PHP 2,500.

A resort quotes PHP 16,000 and a van provider quotes PHP 8,000. The PHP 24,000 total costs PHP 2,400 each while all ten people are joining.

Two people withdraw. The share becomes PHP 3,000, so the plan no longer satisfies the group agreement.

Arat? would identify the broken condition and offer clear choices: request revised quotes, choose another plan, invite more participants, or ask everyone to approve the higher amount.

How the product would work

@switch (selectedStep) { @case ('plan') {

Start with what the group can accept.

Group input
Possible dates, attendance, requirements, preferences, and personal budget limits.
Decision
Separate requirements such as enough beds from preferences such as a karaoke room.
Privacy
Individual budget limits stay private by default. The group controls which range is shared with suppliers.
} @case ('quote') {

Collect offers that can be compared.

Supplier input
Resorts provide capacity, inclusions, deposits, cancellation terms, and quote expiry. Vehicle providers add route, capacity, fuel, toll, parking, and overtime conditions.
Access
A simple invitation link lets a supplier respond without adopting a full management system.
Unknowns
Unresolved costs are marked as needing clarification, not treated as within budget.
} @case ('agree') {

Approve one version of the plan.

Agreement
Participants approve a specific combination of date, headcount, price, inclusions, and conditions.
Versioning
A later change creates a new version instead of silently rewriting what was approved.
Boundary
Group approval does not mean that suppliers confirmed or that payment was received.
} @case ('change') {

Show what changed and what it breaks.

Trigger
Attendance, price, availability, inclusions, or supplier conditions change.
Check
Arat? would recalculate the plan and identify approvals or requirements that are no longer valid.
Resolution
Revise an offer, compare another option, invite participants, or request explicit approval for the new plan.
} }

Product boundaries

Agreement is not a reservation.

Arat? would track group agreement, supplier confirmation, and payment status separately. A quote is not a reservation, and an unexpired quote is not automatically an availability hold.

If the resort confirms while the vehicle provider declines, the outing is partially confirmed, not booked. A payment screenshot may be recorded for coordination, but it does not prove that a supplier received or accepted payment.

Product principle: every status must describe what is known without creating false certainty.

Focused first version

One outing, two supplier types, one shared decision.

The first version would focus on private resort outings with optional van transport. It would include a planning room, supplier quote form, comparison view, conditional approval, quote expiry, cost recalculation, confirmation tracking, and an audit history.

Organizers could also record an offer received outside Arat?, with its source clearly labeled and supplier confirmation still required. This keeps group planning useful before supplier adoption is proven.

Excluded from the first version: payment collection, automatic booking, public reviews, broad travel search, real-time vehicle tracking, and large travel-platform integrations.

Engineering direction

Version the agreement, not just the record.

The proposed implementation is an Angular experience backed by a Java 21 and Spring Boot modular monolith. PostgreSQL would hold planning rooms, participant constraints, supplier offers, plan versions, approvals, confirmations, and audit events.

Planning, suppliers, offers, agreements, and confirmations would have explicit boundaries. A participant or supplier change would run a transactional recalculation against the approved plan version. Version checks would prevent a person from approving stale terms.

Background notifications can be added after the core workflow is proven. RabbitMQ should enter the architecture only when asynchronous delivery has a demonstrated need.

Validation before marketplace growth

The workflow is plausible. Demand is still a hypothesis.

Research must test whether organizers will share structured plans, whether participants will set honest constraints, and whether resort and van operators find these inquiries more useful than a simple price question.

Supplier willingness to respond or pay, viable booking operations, cancellation handling, and customer acquisition are unvalidated. No users, reservations, revenue, partnerships, or production readiness are being claimed.

Future exploration

Broaden only after change detection proves useful.

Later work could explore supplier-managed availability, reservation holds, payments, notifications, caterers, and activity operators. Those additions should follow evidence that groups value the agreement and change-resolution workflow.

Arat? is currently a product and architecture concept. There is no application, public API, supplier network, or production deployment yet.
