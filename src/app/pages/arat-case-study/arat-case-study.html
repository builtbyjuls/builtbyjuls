Product concept - Group planning first

Arat?: plan together before anyone commits.

Arat? is designed for any group plan, from a simple gala to international travel. Groups can coordinate privately and, only if they choose, invite suppliers or publish an approved brief to receive offers.

Role
Product strategy, workflow design, and architecture
Initial focus
Private group planning with optional supplier sourcing
Status
Concept stage - Not implemented

The problem

Group plans fail between "tara" and "booked."

Planning an outing often means scattered chats, private budget concerns, uncertain headcount, and quotes with different conditions. A plan can look settled even when participants approved different dates, prices, or assumptions.

When attendance, dates, costs, options, or supplier conditions change, the organizer must work out who is affected and whether the original agreement still holds.

Core product model

Useful before any supplier joins.

A group can use Arat? only to coordinate itself. Friends planning a gala, reunion, road trip, or international holiday can compare dates, budgets, requirements, and itinerary options, approve one version, then make every booking outside the platform.

Supplier participation is an optional branch controlled by the group. The planning room and private participant information never become a public listing.

Private
No supplier can discover the plan.
Invite-only
The organizer shares an approved brief with selected suppliers or rental providers.
Discoverable
Eligible providers may view the approved brief and submit structured offers.

Privacy rule: suppliers see only what the group approves for sharing, never individual budget limits, private comments, rejected dates, or internal voting history.

The story

Ten friends, one plan, and two withdrawals.

Ten friends want an overnight stay. They agree on two possible weekends, a private pool, enough beds, van transport, and a maximum personal share of PHP 2,500.

A resort quotes PHP 16,000 and a van provider quotes PHP 8,000. The PHP 24,000 total costs PHP 2,400 each while all ten people are joining.

Two people withdraw. The share becomes PHP 3,000, so the plan no longer satisfies the group agreement.

Arat? would identify the broken condition and offer clear choices: request revised quotes, choose another plan, invite more participants, or ask everyone to approve the higher amount.

How the product would work

@switch (selectedStep) { @case ('plan') {

Start with what the group can accept.

Group input
Possible dates, attendance, requirements, preferences, and personal budget limits.
Decision
Separate requirements such as enough beds from preferences such as a karaoke room.
Privacy
Individual budget limits stay private by default. The group controls which range is shared with suppliers.
} @case ('compare') {

Compare plans, with or without suppliers.

Options
The group can compare its own itineraries, organizer-recorded prices, external links, or offers submitted through Arat?.
Optional sourcing
If the group opts in, invited or eligible providers can respond to an approved brief without seeing the private planning room.
Unknowns
Unresolved costs are marked as needing clarification, not treated as within budget.
} @case ('agree') {

Approve one version of the plan.

Agreement
Participants approve a specific combination of date, headcount, price, inclusions, and conditions.
Versioning
A later change creates a new version instead of silently rewriting what was approved.
Boundary
Group approval does not mean that suppliers confirmed or that payment was received.
} @case ('adapt') {

Show what changed and what it breaks.

Trigger
Attendance, dates, price, availability, inclusions, or conditions change.
Check
Arat? would recalculate the plan and identify approvals or requirements that are no longer valid.
Resolution
Revise the plan, compare another option, invite participants, request a new offer, or ask for explicit approval of the changed version.
} }

Product boundaries

Agreement is not a reservation.

Arat? would track group agreement, supplier confirmation, and payment status separately. A quote is not a reservation, and an unexpired quote is not automatically an availability hold.

If the resort confirms while the vehicle provider declines, the outing is partially confirmed, not booked. A payment screenshot may be recorded for coordination, but it does not prove that a supplier received or accepted payment.

Product principle: every status must describe what is known without creating false certainty.

Focused first version

Prove private planning before marketplace growth.

The first version would include private planning rooms, participant invitations, date availability, requirements and preferences, private budget limits, proposed plan versions, cost calculation, participant approval, change detection, and an audit history.

The first vertical slice would create a plan, approve a fixed-cost version, remove one participant, and show why the earlier agreement is no longer valid. Supplier invitations, structured offers, and discoverable briefs would follow as a separate slice.

Excluded from the first version: supplier accounts, public request discovery, payment collection, automatic booking, public reviews, broad travel search, and real-time vehicle tracking.

Engineering direction

Version the agreement, not just the record.

The proposed implementation is an Angular experience backed by a Java 21 and Spring Boot modular monolith. PostgreSQL would hold planning rooms, participant constraints, plan versions, approvals, change impacts, and audit events.

Identity and planning would form the first module boundaries, with agreements initially kept inside planning unless separation becomes useful. A material change would run a transactional recalculation against the approved plan version. Version checks would prevent approval of stale terms.

Supplier sourcing, confirmations, and notifications would be added only after private group planning works. RabbitMQ should enter the architecture only when asynchronous delivery has a demonstrated need.

Validation before expansion

The workflow is plausible. Demand is still a hypothesis.

Research must first test whether organizers want structured participant input, whether people will provide private constraints, and whether groups understand versioned approval and change alerts.

Supplier participation is a separate hypothesis: providers may value complete, structured inquiries more than a simple price question, but their willingness to respond or pay is unvalidated. No users, reservations, revenue, partnerships, or production readiness are being claimed.

Future exploration

Add sourcing only after group planning proves useful.

Later work could add invited supplier offers, group-approved discoverable briefs, provider profiles, supplier-managed availability, reservation holds, payments, notifications, caterers, and activity operators.

Arat? is currently a product and architecture concept. There is no application, public API, supplier network, or production deployment yet.
