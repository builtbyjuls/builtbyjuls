Q-ify: a marketplace for delegating time-consuming, transferable queues.

Q-ify explores how customers can delegate permitted private-sector queues to verified runners, receive controlled progress updates, and arrive when a handoff is ready.

Role
Product strategy, backend architecture, and implementation
Target experience
Future mobile-first customer and runner applications
Status
Phase 4 complete - Not launched

The problem

Waiting costs more than time.

Physical queues can mean missed work, uncertain travel, heat or rain, and difficulty caring for children or relatives. Existing options either move the burden to someone else or work only when a venue has installed its own queue system.

Research and evidence

The behavior exists, but demand remains unproven.

Public research found local queue-management products, paid assistance for online ticket queues, and an informal Metro Manila offer to wait in physical lines. It did not establish transaction volume or prove a viable Philippine marketplace for private-sector queue assistance.

Evidence: phone-first access is credible in the Philippines, while willingness to pay, repeat demand, and venue acceptance are still hypotheses.

Refined product direction

Permitted private-sector queues and on-site errands.

The product vision is a B2C private-sector pasuyo marketplace for delegating time-consuming, transferable queues and authorized on-site errands. It is not an unrestricted proxy service. Venue and organizer rules determine whether a queue, handoff, or purchase is allowed.

  • Limited food and retail product releases
  • Warehouse, clearance, and sample sales
  • Restaurant or event waitlist holding
  • Event merchandise queues
  • In-store availability checks and permitted on-site purchasing
Q-ify does not bypass a line. Venue and organizer policy must permit the assistance and any later handoff.

Product vision

A controlled customer-runner workflow.

In the envisioned marketplace, a customer requests help only for an approved venue and allowed queue type. A verified runner accepts the work, shares limited progress, and gives the customer notice to arrive for a permitted handoff.

Product vision, not market proof: Q-ify has no launched marketplace, venue partners, customers, transactions, or validated commercial demand.

What must be validated

Demand, permission, safety, and economics remain open.

  • Will enough customers pay a price that covers fair runner earnings, travel, support, verification, and payments?
  • Will private venues and organizers give written permission for the intended handoffs?
  • Can customers arrive reliably within a permitted handoff window?
  • Can the service operate without creating safety, labor, privacy, anti-fixer, or anti-scalping problems?

Architecture and engineering decisions

One deployable, with boundaries that tests can enforce.

Q-ify is a Java 21 and Spring Boot modular monolith with PostgreSQL and Flyway persistence. Spring Modulith verifies the module graph: Bookings to Venues, Runners to Venues, Dispatch to Bookings and Runners, and Tasks to Dispatch.

Each module owns its data and exposes application APIs across its boundary. Floci provides selected AWS-compatible services locally, but no AWS adapter or cloud deployment has been verified.

Phase 4 keeps terminal coordination inside the modular monolith. The Tasks module closes a task and its related runner, booking, and assignment using a defined locking order and one recorded outcome time.

Concurrency and transactions

One competing terminal outcome must win.

Opportunity discovery is advisory and does not reserve work. Acceptance locks the runner before the booking and repeats eligibility checks in one transaction, preventing the same runner or booking from being assigned twice under concurrent requests.

Phase 4 serializes competing completion and failure requests so one terminal outcome wins. If releasing the runner fails, the terminal changes roll back together. Progress estimates remain runner-declared, append-only records; they do not claim real-time location, venue, or customer verification.

Honest limitations

A backend demonstration, not an operating marketplace.

Q-ify is a synthetic portfolio implementation. It has no user-facing Q-ify application, authenticated users, payments, notifications, production deployment, real AWS verification, venue operations, or commercial transactions.

The current completion proof demonstrates secret possession only. It does not establish a customer's identity, physical handoff, venue approval, or a transferable entitlement. The unauthenticated APIs are a demo boundary, not an operating control.

Concert-ticket and sneaker-release assistance are only future, policy-sensitive possibilities. Q-ify does not claim to bypass ticket limits, named accounts, venue rules, or anti-scalping protections. Government, immigration, school, bank, medical, biometric, and impersonation scenarios are outside this direction.
