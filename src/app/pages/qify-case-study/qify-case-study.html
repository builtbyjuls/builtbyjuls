Product concept - Validation stage

Q-ify: trusted help for allowed physical queues.

A proposed Philippine marketplace connecting customers who cannot remain in line with verified runners who can hold an ordinary, transferable place at an approved venue.

Role
Product research and system planning
Platform
Mobile-first web
Status
Not launched

The problem

Waiting costs more than time.

Physical queues can mean missed work, uncertain travel, heat or rain, and difficulty caring for children or relatives. Existing options either move the burden to someone else or work only when a venue has installed its own queue system.

Research and evidence

The behavior exists, but demand remains unproven.

Public research found local queue-management products, paid assistance for online ticket queues, and an informal Metro Manila offer to wait in physical lines. It did not establish transaction volume or prove a viable Philippine marketplace for physical queue handoffs.

Evidence: phone-first access is credible in the Philippines, while willingness to pay, repeat demand, and venue acceptance are still hypotheses.

The scope correction

The responsible product is narrower than the original idea.

Government transactions, clinics, and protected-priority queues introduce serious policy, identity, privacy, and fairness risks. The proposed first version supports only ordinary private-venue queues where handoffs are explicitly permitted.

Q-ify is not a way to bypass a line. It is a transparent handoff of an ordinary queue position where the venue allows it.

Proposed experience

A controlled handoff, not an unrestricted errand.

  1. The customer books only at an approved venue and reviews its handoff rules.
  2. A verified runner checks in and posts limited status updates.
  3. The customer receives enough notice to travel before the queue reaches a nontransferable checkpoint.
  4. Both parties confirm the handoff with a rotating PIN or QR code.

What must be validated

Trust and economics come before automation.

  • Will enough customers pay a price that covers fair runner earnings, travel, support, verification, and payments?
  • Will private venues give written permission for queue handoffs?
  • Can customers arrive reliably within the handoff window?
  • Can the service operate without creating safety, labor, privacy, or anti-fixer problems?

Possible technical direction

Start with a modular monolith.

If validation succeeds, the proposed foundation is a mobile-first Angular application, a Java 21 and Spring Boot backend, PostgreSQL, REST APIs, and background jobs for alerts and task deadlines. A modular monolith keeps operational cost low while the workflow is still changing.

Proposed design: this is a direction, not a built system.

Next step

Run a concierge pilot before building the marketplace.

The next investment is not a native app. It is customer, runner, and venue research followed by a tightly monitored manual pilot. Product development should begin only after demand, permission, safety, payment flow, and contribution margin pass predefined gates.

Research notes

Sources behind the early direction.

The public scan was conducted on September 1, 2026. It supports the problem framing, not proof of demand, market size, or completed transactions.

  • Philippine queue-management products: ezpila, Pumila.ph, and QueueMate
